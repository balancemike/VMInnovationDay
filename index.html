02 · What the session taught us

Ten things the session taught us

We looked for patterns across the eight boards of Post-it notes and counted the notes behind each one. Each learning ends with a quick test. The tests are small on purpose, so you can learn something real in days or weeks without committing to a product.

12 notes

People kept describing a Keurig.

A Keurig is a coffee maker that works at the push of one button. Twelve notes on six boards asked for something like it, using phrases like “dump and go,” “idiot proof” and “set it and forget it.” One note even suggested giving up some performance to make the machine more convenient. That pulls against Vitamix’s identity, which is built on power and control.

From the researchVitamix’s own research found that owners struggle to pick the right settings.

Quick test

Cover the dial on a few blenders so that only one program button is left. Lend them to ten owners for a week.

10 owners1 weekExisting machine

You’ll learn whether fewer choices lead to more use, and you’ll hear what owners miss.

11 notes

People want someone else to decide what to make.

Eleven notes on five boards asked for help with the choice itself. The ideas included a recipe roulette, a daily blend suggestion on a QR code, and recipes built from what’s already in the fridge. Different tables came up with these on their own.

Quick test

Place a small card next to the machine with one suggestion each morning. Leave it there for two weeks with 15 owners.

15 owners2 weeksPrinted cards

You’ll learn whether a single suggestion gets the machine used more than a full recipe library does.

8 of 8

Every board wanted someone else to handle the ingredients.

Ideas about getting food to the owner showed up on all eight boards, across 19 notes. They included meal kits, frozen packs that are ready to blend, produce delivery, and sending a recipe’s ingredients straight to an online grocery order. Together they suggest the room sees prep, more than blending, as the real barrier.

From the researchIn the research, 16% of owners said having to prep ingredients first frustrates them.

Quick test

Buy frozen smoothie or soup packs from a grocery store and add a label that says which program to use. Give them to 15 owners for three weeks.

15 owners3 weeksStore bought packs

You’ll learn whether taking prep off the owner’s plate does more than another recipe would.

14 notes

The room saw the blender as something people use together.

Fourteen notes on five boards brought other people into the kitchen. Kids who want to use the blender came up several times. So did family recipes, cooking as a couple, making a dip together and a party night. Most thinking about usage focuses on one person, but the room kept adding more.

Quick test

Send five families a simple weekend kit. Include three recipes kids can make on their own, plus a scorecard.

5 families1 weekend

You’ll learn whether a second person in the kitchen brings the machine out more often.

11 notes

Some people wanted a real person to teach them.

Eleven notes on four boards asked for help from a person. The ideas included cooking classes at the library, home parties where a host shows the product to friends, demos in stores like Costco, a chef you can call, and local clubs led by experienced owners. This stood out because most of the room reached for apps and videos.

From the researchVitamix grew through live demonstrations. In 1949, a single half hour TV demo sold more than 400 machines.

Quick test

Host one live video cook along with ten new owners. Compare what they make over the next month with owners who didn’t join.

10 owners1 session4 week follow up

You’ll learn whether a real person does more than a video to get owners past their first try.

6 notes

Nobody agreed on the right size.

Some notes asked for smaller portions, for people who live alone or for making dips. Another asked for a container big enough to make smoothies for a family of five or six. The standard large container may be the wrong size for both.

From the researchVitamix already sells smaller blending cups and bowls that fit on the same motor base. Many owners of the full size machine may not have them.

Quick test

Give the small cups and bowls to 15 owners who only have the large container. Track what they make over a month.

15 owners4 weeksExisting accessories

You’ll learn whether the right size changes how often the machine gets used.

10 notes

To fight forgetting, the room reached for reminders.

Ten notes suggested ways to remind owners to use the machine. They included emails, phone notifications, daily streaks and an alert when you walk into a grocery store. A few notes did put something on the machine itself, but those were recipe ideas rather than prompts to use it.

From the researchIn the research, Vitamix owners were more likely than other blender owners to say the machine is heavy and hard to move.

Quick test

Split 20 owners into two groups for three weeks. One group gets a weekly phone reminder, and the other gets a card or small light on the counter next to the machine.

20 owners3 weeks

You’ll learn whether a cue in the kitchen works better than a message on the phone.

4 boards

A few people raised how it feels to own a Vitamix.

One note suggested a usage counter on the machine, so owners could see the cost paying off. Others suggested renting, renting to own, or a more affordable option. Each idea appeared on a single note, but together they came from four different boards.

From the researchVitamix offers financing and certified reconditioned machines today. We didn’t find a rental program.

Quick test

Send 20 owners a weekly note showing what each use has cost them so far. Watch whether their use changes over four weeks.

20 owners4 weeksEmail only

You’ll learn whether seeing the value builds the habit or just adds guilt.

2.6 vs 1.5

The room agreed on guidance but split on the machine.

We counted how many notes mentioned each idea. Ideas about guidance and content were mentioned 2.6 times each on average, and ideas about the machine 1.5 times. In other words, many people suggested the same content ideas, while almost everyone had a different idea for the machine.

Quick test

Give one group from across Vitamix the 23 ideas that would improve the current machine. Ask them to pick the three that matter most.

1 group1 hour

You’ll learn whether people share a view of the machine’s main problem, and who sees it the same way.

4 questions

Some people wanted facts before ideas.

Four notes were questions rather than ideas. One asked how people actually eat dinner, and another simply said “Takeout >50%.” The other two asked for a comparison of how people really use the machine and for a way to measure the return. These people were asking for information that wasn’t in the room.

From the researchThe research summary shared that day had only one slide focused on Vitamix owners.

Quick test

Email a few hundred registered owners one question. Ask what they made last week and what almost stopped them.

300 owners1 weekEmail only

You’ll have the start of the usage picture the room was asking for.

Worth noting

What the boards didn’t say

7 notes

Cleaning is the biggest barrier in Vitamix’s research, at 25% of owners. It came up on only seven notes.

2 notes

Noise is tied for the top complaint in the research, at 22%. It came up on only two notes.

0 notes

Baby food, nut butters and plant milk show the widest gap between owners who know they could make them and owners who do. No note mentioned them.

The ideas are already there. The next move is learning which ones matter most.

Each test is small on purpose. The goal is to learn quickly and cheaply before anyone commits to a product. A companion readout lists the source behind every figure.
